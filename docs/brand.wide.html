Year One
Months 1–12

Year One · Build Your Foundation

You select the year-one scope; Thanksgiving delivery remains a separate proposal.

  • Choose whether to authorize a local-search pilot, keep the baseline, and review outcomes at 30, 60, and 90 days.
  • Collect permission-based email signups from customers who want updates about your products and market appearances.
  • Approve a library of farm photographs and a founder story that represents you and your team accurately.
  • Share the documented managed-grazing project without asserting certifications that have not been verified.
  • Consider a holiday delivery pilot only after you approve product availability, compliance, cold-chain controls, capacity, and economics.
Year Two
Months 13–24

Year Two · Build Repeat Demand

The goal is more dependable relationships, not more channels for their own sake.

  • Consider recurring pickup or ordering for products you can supply reliably.
  • Test interest in a meat share or seasonal box only after reviewing contribution margin.
  • Explore wholesale inquiries if your processing, packaging, price, and fulfillment capacity can support them.
  • Send a farm update at a cadence you can maintain and customers have agreed to receive.
  • Offer educational visits or internship activities only if you and your family want them and can manage them safely.
Year Three
Months 25–36

Year Three · Expand If Earned

You remain free to delay, change, or decline any expansion.

  • Add another processor only if your current capacity is a proven bottleneck and the cost makes sense.
  • Hire marketing or fulfillment help only when repeat-order contribution can cover it.
  • Develop educational opportunities within the supervision and time you can provide.
  • Test adjacent products after demand for your current products is established.
  • Set your own minimum profitability and operating thresholds before authorizing new sales channels.
Years Four & Five
Months 37–60 · Conditional

Years Four & Five · Keep Your Options

No later-year facility, expansion, or expenditure is presumed to have your approval.

  • Use repeat rate, cash generation, and farm economics to decide whether a broader delivery area is desirable.
  • Treat any Nashville location, wholesale relationship, or pickup point as a separate investment to evaluate.
  • Keep family workload, land stewardship, and succession on a timetable you control.
  • Choose whether to publish soil, habitat, or grazing measurements after methods and results have been verified.
What Sustainability Means

Your Definition of Sustainability

Ecological

Protect soil, forage, and water while choosing what projects or measured outcomes you are comfortable documenting publicly.

Economic

Give each channel a financial test so growth does not consume resources better used on your existing operation.

Purpose

Let your faith, educational work, and community relationships inform the way you operate rather than turning them into unverified promotional claims.
