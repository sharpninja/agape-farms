The Decision

Decisions Only You Can Approve

Before any additional delivery is advertised, you should confirm the actual birds, processor release timing, and how customer orders would be fulfilled.

  • Confirm your available birds, expected weights, current $20 deposit arrangement, processing schedule, and inspection or exemption status.
  • Decide whether to authorize a delivery arrangement covering fees, addresses, refunds, delays, insurance, responsibility, and customer contact.
  • Check travel from the actual pickup point against refrigeration capacity, driver availability, and realistic delivery windows.
  • Verify eligibility for any Kentucky or Alabama sales before even considering offering service across state lines.
  • Authorize only specific routes, deadlines, product allocations, and the person responsible for each delivery.

October 24–31 · Validate

First, Confirm Your Constraints

  • Review the deposits you already offer, reserved bird counts, processing release times, and current customer pickup arrangements.
  • Estimate stops possible per vehicle using actual travel, loading, refrigeration, and handoff times.
  • Verify the applicable inspection or exemption rules, state restrictions, and insurance obligations.
  • Agree on delivery charges, cancellations, substitutions, customer support, and contingencies before launching anything.
If your processing schedule cannot support multiple delivery days, the proposed radius should not be advertised as available.
02
November 1–10 · Conditional Planning

Open Only Approved Routes

  • Publish only the delivery locations, fees, deadlines, and arrival windows you have specifically approved.
  • Keep your existing $20 turkey deposit distinct from any later weight-based balance and optional delivery fee.
  • Limit bookings to birds and delivery slots you can actually fulfill; waitlist additional interest rather than promise capacity.
  • Confirm the route and date to each customer before taking any additional delivery payment.
The November 10 planning cutoff is only a proposal. Your actual order deadline remains your decision.
03
November 11–24 · If Approved

Deliver Under an Agreed Plan

Your processing, fulfillment, and approval requirements determine whether any added delivery route runs.

  • Confirm your processor has released each bird and document product handoff to the delivery operator.
  • Use suitable food-safe, validated refrigeration capable of keeping the product at or below 40°F during transit.
  • Record loading, in-transit, and handoff temperatures; document recipients and exceptions.
  • Provide delivery windows, an operator contact, and a clear plan for delays or temperature-control failures.
  • Invite customers to share honest feedback after their orders arrive.
Why Test It

Why Test a Small Route First?

Your Existing Reservations

Your turkey-deposit listing gives us a place to start measuring demand without pretending that all customers want expanded delivery.

Your Control of the Sale

You retain responsibility for authorizing sales and product release; Payton's proposed role would be limited to deliveries agreed in writing.

Better Route Economics

Grouping nearby stops could reduce costs, but only when it preserves your product quality and dependable customer handoff.
