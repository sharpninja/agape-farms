The Decision

What Must Be Agreed

Confirm product availability and processing release before publishing any new customer delivery promises.

  • Verify bird count, expected weights, existing $20 deposit terms, processor release time, and inspection or exemption status.
  • Define a written service scope — route fees, accepted addresses, insurance, refunds, delays, liability, and customer communications.
  • Plan from the actual pickup point with realistic drive time, refrigeration capacity, and customer handoff windows—not radius alone.
  • Confirm each state and sales channel is legally eligible before accepting orders for Kentucky or Alabama destinations.
  • Dispatch only when the farm has approved the route, cutoff, product allocation, and responsible operator.

01
October 24–31 · Validate

Confirm Product and Dispatch

  • Confirm turkey deposits already offered by the farm, product count, processor release times, and customer pickup commitments.
  • Measure the number of safe deliveries possible per vehicle and route, including loading, drive time, stops, and temperature control.
  • Verify inspection or exemption status and applicable state-specific transport and sales requirements.
  • Agree on fees, liability, order cutoffs, substitutions, refunds, support, and contingency arrangements.
Decision gate: if fresh birds cannot be released early enough, do not advertise multiple corridor days or a blanket 200-mile promise.
02
November 1–10 · Conditional Planning

Accept Only Deliverable Orders

  • Publish only farm-approved addresses, delivery fees, quantity and weight terms, deadlines, and customer arrival windows.
  • Link to the farm's existing turkey reservation, clearly separating its $20 deposit from the final weight-based charge and any delivery fee.
  • Close or waitlist a corridor when confirmed allocations, refrigeration capacity, or route timing are exhausted.
  • Notify each buyer of the validated route and delivery date before collecting a separate delivery payment.
November 10 is a proposed planning cutoff, not a verified farm deadline; do not publish it as policy without approval.
03
November 11–24 · If Approved

Dispatch and Document

The final route schedule remains contingent on confirmed product release and service capacity.

  • Obtain processing-release and product handoff documentation before loading.
  • Use suitable food-safe transport, maintaining refrigerated product at 40°F or below with a validated temperature-control method.
  • Record temperatures at loading, during transit, and at handoff; log delivery times, recipients, and any exception.
  • Provide realistic arrival windows, an operator contact, and a documented response to delays or loss of refrigeration.
  • Request optional, unbiased customer feedback only after successful fulfillment.
Why Test It

Why Pilot First

Existing Interest

The farm's turkey deposit product provides a real starting point for testing the number and location of interested buyers.

Clear Responsibility

The farm controls sales, product and processing decisions; the delivery operator serves only documented, accepted routes under written terms.

Consolidated Stops

Grouping stops may lower cost per delivery, but only where it protects timing, product temperature, and service quality.
